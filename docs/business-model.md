# Business model notes

## Nonprofit vs small business

- A nonprofit can legally pay a market salary (e.g. $180k for a forward deployed engineer) if it's "reasonable": comparable to similar orgs, approved by an independent board in advance, documented with comparability data at the time of the vote. Excess pay triggers IRS excise taxes (25% of the excess, 200% if not corrected). California also requires the board to approve executive pay as just and reasonable. Pay is public on Form 990.
- Practical issue: a new org paying its founder a high salary before it has revenue makes funders nervous.
- **Decision for now: small business (LLC).** Simpler, faster, full control. Shelter software vendors (Shelterluv, PetPoint) are for-profit too.

## Pricing

- Cover Eric's time plus a margin.
- Sliding scale: full price for larger or better-funded shelters, discounted for small rescues.
- Fiscal sponsorship is an option later if grant funding (Maddie's Fund, Petco Love, etc.) becomes important.

## Funding small shelters

- The **shelter** runs the fundraiser (GoFundMe charity fundraiser pays the nonprofit directly; donations are deductible; shelters have donor lists), then pays our invoice.
- If we ever run a campaign ourselves as a business: donations aren't deductible, the money is taxable income, and the description must say plainly that it pays for our services to that shelter.
- Offer a **fundraising kit**: short blurb, dollar goal, photos/stats of what the project does for the animals.
- Kuranda's ShelterBeds wish-list program is a good model to study.

Not legal or financial advice. Talk to a lawyer/accountant when forming the entity.
